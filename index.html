KCC Mesh · the compute-economy rail

In plain words: a ledger, kept and checked in your browser, of who did which piece of work and who made which release. It counts in KCC, the estate’s internal contribution unit — a unit of credit for work that re-checks, not money. “The compute-economy rail” is this ledger and its rules: work is credited only when someone re-runs it and gets the same answer.

Idle rigs stop mining wasted hashes and become sovereign mesh-nodes that earn internal contribution-units by verified work: a bounty is escrowed, a node claims it and does the compute, and it is paid only if the result re-verifies (proof-of-contribution, not proof-of-asking). Units are conserved — no minting after genesis — on a tamper-evident chain, and each node has a budget ceiling it cannot cross, so no node can drain the mesh.

⚠ Internal contribution-units — NOT money. These are not a currency, not a cryptocurrency, not tradeable. There is no code path from a unit to money: this kernel exposes no fiat / trade / exchange / withdraw / sell / convert verb (a test checks that against the code). The money rail is a separate, gated matter behind legal counsel.

✓ compute rail · this page

Built here. A machine joins as a node, claims a posted job, does it, and is credited internal units only if the result re-runs to the same answer. Every step is an entry on a chain anyone can replay. No central server and no money: units never leave the ledger.

✗ money rail · not built

Turning units into anything tradeable — a currency, a way in or out of money, an exchange listing — would bring in securities, money-transmission, financial-promotion, tax and employment law. None of it is built, and none of it will be without legal advice first and a person’s decision.

The contribution ledger

Every release, recorded as a signed contribution — internal contribution units, NOT money. A release (a DIGI card, an app, a node) is written signed by its maker; ownership moves by a give its owner of the moment signs; a measured contribution (a card’s leave-one-out share of a mesh’s work) is written signed by a named measurer. Nothing counts unless its gate re-runs. The chain is append-only and replays from nothing. The allocation switch is off: this kernel computes sandbox allocations for anyone, has no verb that turns the switch on, and none that moves anything out of the ledger. Whether an allocation is ever worth anything is a decision for the ledger’s owner, with legal counsel — not this code.

For an organisation

Who made what, who holds it, and what it measurably added — on a record nobody can quietly rewrite, and a fair split of any pool you choose, that anyone can re-derive. Teams ship tools, models and analyses that others build on. Credit for that is usually decided in a meeting from memory. The contribution ledger records it at the moment of release, signed by whoever made it, and lets a measured contribution count only when its proof re-runs.

What it does

Records each release (with its proofs), each hand-over, and each measured contribution, signed and chained. Computes, from that record alone, each contributor’s part of a pool you name, with every line behind it.

What it does not do

It moves nothing. It is not a payment system, a currency or a token, and it promises no one anything. The pool, the switch and what a part is worth are your organisation’s decisions, under your own policies and advice.

For each role

  • Chief executive: credit follows measured work, not visibility. The record shows who built what the business runs on, from the day it was released.
  • Finance: any allocation you choose to make — a recognition pool, internal credits — re-derives from the ledger to the unit, with every line behind every part. Auditors re-run it; nothing rests on a spreadsheet someone edited.
  • Operations: hand-overs are signed by the holder of the moment, so ownership of every tool and model is current; a second hand-over of the same thing is refused.
  • Technology and security: Ed25519 signatures over one canonical byte form, SHA-256 chaining, append-only, replay from nothing; a gate re-runs each contribution’s proof before it counts. The kernel is mutation-gated on every change, and it exposes no verb that moves anything out.
  • The people who contribute: each can open the ledger and check their own part, and everyone else’s, from the same record — no black box.

Roll-out

  1. Day one: open this page and replay the DIGI drop’s ledger here; read the code (contrib.mjs, one file).
  2. Week one: write your own genesis — your measurers, your activators, your policy (maker’s part, parents’ part, an early weight) — and record each release your teams make, with the proof your own gate re-runs.
  3. Ongoing: keep the ledger where you keep records; anyone given a copy replays it. Compute sandbox allocations whenever you want to see where credit falls. Turn the switch on only when your organisation has decided what a pool is, with the advice it needs.

Risks, answered straight

  • Can a measurer inflate a value? A value counts only if your gate re-runs its proof; a measurer is a key your genesis names, so a bad one is visible and removable in your next genesis.
  • Can a record be changed later? Not without breaking its signature and the chain from that entry on; the tamper test above shows it.
  • Is the policy fair? It is whatever you seal at genesis, before any record it weighs, and it is on the record for everyone to read. The DIGI ledger here carries a demo policy.
  • What about money? This code moves none and promises none. If your organisation ever attaches value to a part, that is a decision with legal and tax dimensions: take advice first.
